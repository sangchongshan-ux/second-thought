Before you start

Three problems you cannot look up

Each problem has an answer that looks obvious and is wrong. You can ask for help, but the help never hands you the answer — it gives you the next step and lets you finish the reasoning.

How this works

  • You answer, and you are told whether you were right — not what the right answer is.
  • If you are stuck you can ask for a hint. Hints come in three levels and you choose when to go deeper.
  • A short reflection appears after you submit, never while you are still working.
  • At the end you get a summary of how you used help, with its limits stated.

What is recorded

Attempts, the hint levels you request, time on each problem and your reflection lines. Nothing else — no keystrokes, no pointer path, no page outside this one.

Recorded in this browser only. You can export or delete it from the summary screen. If you turn this off, the summary is still computed but nothing is stored after you close the tab.

No account, no network requests, no model API. Everything runs in this page.

    What is real in this prototype

    The report distinguishes implemented, simulated and planned work. This panel is the same distinction, in the product itself.

    Implemented
    Problem set, answer checking, three-level hint ladder, lure detection, boundary-timed reflection, session summary with uncertainty statement, local logging, export and delete.
    Simulated
    The hint selector is a rule set (attempt count, time on task, whether the answer matched the item's lure). There is no language model behind it, so hint wording is authored per problem rather than generated.
    Planned
    Model-generated hints, a tutor dashboard, and a delayed retest that would let the summary say something about retention rather than about one sitting.
